# Weather-Aware Staffing Policy

## Hot Weather

For dessert, ice cream, and food retail businesses, hot weather can increase cold-item demand. When the forecast high is above 85 degrees Fahrenheit, ShiftIQ may recommend one extra coverage slot if the weather-adjusted forecast crosses a staffing threshold.

## Rain Risk

Rain can reduce walk-in traffic, but it may not fully offset hot-weather demand. If both heat and rain are present, ShiftIQ should explain the mixed signal and avoid aggressive cuts unless sales history also indicates slow demand.

## Confidence

Weather adjustments should be treated as recommendations, not guarantees. Managers should compare weather-adjusted staffing to historical POS demand and labor guardrails before approving major schedule changes.

## Auditability

Weather-driven staffing recommendations should cite the weather condition, high temperature, precipitation risk, baseline staff level, and adjusted staff level.
